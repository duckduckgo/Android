# App Tracking Protection

The user turns on App Tracking Protection (AppTP) from Settings. The first time, three onboarding pages end on
"Enable App Tracking Protection", Android asks for VPN consent, and the app says "Good news! App Tracking
Protection is now enabled". AppTP then runs as a local VPN that blocks trackers in other apps. Its screen shows
the blocked attempts and has a switch to turn it off, a list of apps with a protection switch each, report
forms for app issues, and "Disable and Delete Data". Settings, the New Tab Page and an ongoing notification
show whether it is on.

AppTP blocks another app's connection to a tracker domain by the server name (SNI) in its TLS ClientHello:
netguard (`netguard-android`, read by `NgVpnNetworkStack`) parses the SNI from the connection's first segment
and drops the connection when the blocklist names that domain for that app. The app itself, system apps, apps
on the exclusion lists and apps without a label are never blocked, so the blocking proof uses its own probe app,
"Verify Probe" (`probe/`, `$V probe`).

## Sub-features

- `apptp-settings-entry` Settings > App Tracking Protection; the row's status reads "On" or "Off".
- `apptp-onboarding` until the user has been through it, the row opens three pages ("One easy step for better app privacy!", "How does it work?", "Who sees your data?"). The last page's button reads "Enable App Tracking Protection".
- `apptp-vpn-consent` Android's "Connection request" dialog (OK / Cancel), shown until the app has VPN consent.
- `apptp-enabled` the tracker activity screen with the switch on and "DuckDuckGo is blocking hidden trackers across your apps.". A VPN network is up, with a `tun` interface.
- `apptp-empty-feed` with no history, the feed reads "Blocked tracking attempts will appear here", with 0 blocked across 0 apps.
- `apptp-reenable` after onboarding, the row opens the tracker screen, and its switch turns AppTP back on.
- `apptp-ntp-card` the New Tab Page card reads "App Tracking Protection is enabled and blocking..." (or, with blocked trackers in the history, "App Tracking Protection blocked N tracking attempts in ...") or "App Tracking Protection disabled.". Tapping it opens the tracker screen.
- `apptp-app-list` "View Apps" (My Apps) has a protection switch per app. Turning one off asks "Report an issue?", and the app then reads "Manually disabled".
- `apptp-report` the report form ("What's happening?" categories, a text field, Submit). It opens from that dialog's "Report Issue", or from My Apps' menu > "Report an Issue With an App" > pick an app > Next.
- `apptp-having-issues` "Having Issues?" opens recommended system settings and the recent app activity.
- `apptp-remove` "Disable and Delete Data" asks "Disable and delete data?" (Cancel / Disable).
- `apptp-disable` the switch asks "Disable App Tracking Protection?" with "Disable for One App", "Disable for All Apps" and "Cancel". Disabling for all apps takes the VPN down.
- `apptp-notification` the ongoing "App Tracking Protection" notification opens the tracker screen.
- `apptp-blocking` another app's connections to tracker hosts time out with the SNI anywhere in the first segment (`single`, `window`, `split`), while a non-tracker host goes through.
- `apptp-blocking-late-sni` a tracker whose SNI arrives only in the second segment goes through: netguard reads only the first segment. A known limitation.
- `apptp-blocking-like-moto` `window` with the moto g(60)'s `rmem_max` (needs adbd already running as root), the device setting behind the netguard < 1.10.3 bug (Gotchas).
- `apptp-blocking-feed` after blocking, the tracker screen's feed has a row "N tracking attempts blocked from M companies in Verify Probe app"; it opens `AppTPCompanyTrackersActivity`, "Just Now", listing the blocked companies (Adjust, Branch Metrics).
- `apptp-in-netp` with the VPN (NetP) on too, trackers are blocked inside NetP's WireGuard tunnel, where `GoBackend` asks the app per ClientHello. That includes the hello the kernel retransmits after the app closed its socket, whose owner the kernel no longer knows (uid 0); `GoBackend` uses the owner it saw earlier for that connection.
- `apptp-dev-settings` Settings > Internal Features > "AppTP dev settings" (internal builds) has exception rules, the blocklist, app protections, VPN Debug Logging and the bugreport.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Settings > Protections > "App Tracking Protection" (opens onboarding or the tracker screen, depending on whether the user has onboarded).
- The New Tab Page's AppTP card (`deviceShieldCtaLayout`), shown once AppTP has been onboarded.
- The ongoing notification while AppTP is on.
- On the tracker screen: the toolbar switch, "View Apps", "Having Issues?", "Disable and Delete Data", and, while disabled, the "Report Issues" link in the info panel.
- My Apps: the per-app switches and the "More options" menu ("Restore Default Protected Apps", "Report an Issue With an App").
- Settings > Internal Features > "AppTP dev settings".
- Blocking has no entry point of its own: it happens to another app's traffic while AppTP is on. Its results show in the tracker screen's feed, whose rows open the app's trackers.

## Driving it with verify.sh

Preconditions: baseline (no Web Content Debugging needed), with AppTP off and the VPN (NetP) off: NetP's network is also the app's VPN, so the proofs couldn't tell AppTP's state, and they stop with an error while Settings' VPN row reads On. The proofs don't clear app data, but on the user's own device ask first, because they change it: AppTP ends off whatever it was before, even when a run stops half-way; VPN consent is reset and then granted; Verify Probe is installed and left installed; one protected app's protection is turned off and back on (restored even when the run stops half-way); and `rmem_max` is set only when adbd already runs as root, then restored. To see onboarding again, clear the app's data (only on an emulator the run booted, or with the user's consent). `adb shell appops set com.duckduckgo.mobile.android.debug ACTIVATE_VPN default` makes Android ask for VPN consent again. The proof does this every run and accepts the dialog, so consent ends as `allow`.

- **Entry and onboarding.** Settings (`$V scroll up` until `text:App Tracking Protection` is visible). `$V text` lists the row as `primaryText App Tracking Protection` followed by `label Off`. `$V tap "text:App Tracking Protection"` and `$V where` prints `VpnOnboardingActivity`. Tap `id:onboarding_next_cta` twice ("Continue"), waiting 1.5 s each time for the pager. `$V text onboarding_next_cta` then reads "Enable App Tracking Protection"; tap it.
- **VPN consent.** `$V wait "text:Connection request"` (`$V where` prints `-`, the dialog is Android's), then `$V tap id:button1` (OK).
- **Enabled.** On onboarding, `$V wait "text~:App Tracking Protection is now enabled"`, then `$V tap id:primaryCta` ("Got it!"). On `DeviceShieldTrackerActivity`, `$V checked deviceShieldTrackerSwitch` is `true` and `$V text deviceShieldTrackerBlockingTrackersDescription` reads "DuckDuckGo is blocking hidden trackers across your apps.". Second view: `adb shell dumpsys connectivity` has a `ni{VPN` network agent on `tun<N>` whose `OwnerUid` is the app's uid (`adb shell pm list packages -U com.duckduckgo.mobile.android.debug`; `steps.vpn_up`, so another app's VPN doesn't count), and `adb shell ip -o link` lists the `tun` interface. Pixels after `mark()`: `m_vpn_ev_start_attempt_success_c`, and `m_atp_ev_enabled_onboarding_c` (onboarding) or `m_atp_ev_enabled_tracker_activity_c` (switch). Back once: the Settings row's `label` reads "On". `$V text deviceShieldCtaHeader` on the New Tab Page starts "App Tracking Protection is enabled".
- **Empty feed.** On a fresh app, `activity_description` reads "Blocked tracking attempts will appear here", and both `content_text` read 0. With history, `activity_message` rows replace it ("30 tracking attempts blocked from 2 companies in ... app").
- **App list.** On the tracker screen, `$V scroll up 3`, then `$V tap "text:View Apps"` (title "My Apps", `TrackingProtectionExclusionListActivity`). Rows pair `deviceShieldAppEntryName` (plus an optional `deviceShieldAppExclusionReason`) with `deviceShieldAppEntryShieldEnabled`. Read each with `$V checked deviceShieldAppEntryShieldEnabled <n>`. Tap a checked one: the dialog `trackingProtectionTitle` "Report an issue?" names the app in `trackingProtectionAppLabel` and offers `trackingProtectionExcludeAppDialogSkip` ("Not Now") and `trackingProtectionExcludeAppDialogReport` ("Report Issue"). Either way, the app is now off and its reason reads "Manually disabled". Second view: back on the tracker screen, "N of your apps have protection disabled" (`activity_apps`) is one higher. With none disabled that row is gone and only "N of your apps are protected from hidden trackers ..." shows, followed by "About" (`TrackerFeedAdapter`); the proof reads that as 0. Tapping the switch again turns the app back on with no dialog (Calendar on 2026-11-17).
- **Report.** "Report Issue" opens `ReportBreakageCategorySingleChoiceActivity`. `$V tap "text:What's happening?"` lists seven categories in a dialog. `$V tap "text:App freezes or crashes"`, then `$V tap text:OK`: the list closes and the form's first `internal_edit_text` (hint "What's happening?") reads the category. `layout.py` reads every application window, so while the list is open its text matches too; `ctaNextFormSubmit` reads "Submit". From My Apps: `$V tap "desc:More options"`, then `$V tap "text:Report an Issue With an App"` (`ReportBreakageAppListActivity`, pixel `m_atp_imp_report_breakage_c`). Select an app with `id:deviceShieldInstalledAppSelector --index <n>` in the `deviceShieldInstalledAppEntryName` order, then `$V tap id:ctaSubmitAppBreakage` ("Next") to reach the same form. Stop before Submit (see Gotchas).
- **Having Issues / Disable and Delete Data.** On the tracker screen, `$V scroll up 3`. `$V tap "text:Having Issues?"` opens `ManageRecentAppsProtectionActivity` ("Recommended System Settings", "Recent App Activity"). `$V tap "text:Disable and Delete Data"` opens "Disable and delete data?"; `$V tap text:Cancel` keeps AppTP on.
- **Disable.** `$V tap id:deviceShieldTrackerSwitch` opens `stackedAlertDialogTitle` "Disable App Tracking Protection?". `$V tap text:Cancel` keeps it on. Tap the switch again, then `$V tap "text:Disable for All Apps"`. The switch is `false`, the description reads "Re-enable App Tracking Protection to continue blocking trackers across your apps.", and `infoPanelText` reads "App Tracking Protection is disabled ... Report Issues". The VPN network and `tun` interface are gone, and the pixel is `m_atp_ev_disabled_tracker_activity_c`. Second views: the Settings row reads "Off", and the New Tab Page card reads "App Tracking Protection disabled.".
- **Notification.** With AppTP on, `adb shell cmd statusbar expand-notifications`. The "App Tracking Protection" notification is in a system window, which `$V` selectors don't read. Find its centre with `android layout --device <serial> --flat` (`title` "App Tracking Protection"), tap it with `adb shell input tap x y`, and `$V where` prints `DeviceShieldTrackerActivity`.
- **Blocking.** `$V probe <host,host...> [single|window|split|late-sni] [timeout_ms]` builds the probe with the SDK tools (no Gradle; cached in `build/verify/builds/probe-<hash of probe/>.apk`), installs it when missing or different, starts it and prints one `RESULT ... host=<h> mode=<m> outcome=<o>` line per host; it exits 1 when the run didn't finish. Outcomes: `ALLOWED` (the server replied), `TIMEOUT` (netguard dropped it: blocked), `NO_ADDRESS` (the name didn't resolve, nothing sent), `CLOSED`/`RESET`, and `CONNECT_TIMEOUT`/`CONNECT_FAILED` (never connected, so it says nothing about AppTP; `steps.probe` stops with an error). The proof (`proofs/app_tracking_protection_blocking.py`) probes `app.adjust.com`, `api2.branch.io` (trackers) and `example.com` (control) once with AppTP off, where all are `ALLOWED`, turns AppTP on through the UI (View Apps needs onboarding done), makes sure Verify Probe is protected in My Apps (after turning it back on, it leaves My Apps, which restarts the VPN, and waits for `m_vpn_ev_start_attempt_success_c` and the VPN), then probes each mode from its own `mark()`. "Blocked" is `TIMEOUT` **and** `shouldAllowDomain for <host> (<probe uid>) = AppTracker(...)` in logcat (`NgVpnNetworkStack`), with the uid from `pm list packages -U dev.verifyandroid.probe`. Expectations: `single`, `window`, `split` blocked; `late-sni` allowed; the control allowed every time. When adbd already runs as root (`adb shell id -u` prints 0) it also runs `window` at `rmem_max` 134217728 and restores the old value; otherwise `pr-summary` lists `window like moto` as not verified. The tunnel must still be up at the end. Each mode's NetGuard.JNI, NgVpnNetworkStack and VerifyProbe lines go to `apptp-netguard-<mode>.txt` in the evidence (`TLS server <host> (<ip>) found` is the SNI netguard parsed), and the report's Modes table has each mode's window scales (`new session mss N ws S`) and first segment sizes (`/443 seq 1 ack N data B`). On this emulator: ws 6 and a 609-byte first segment for `window`, ws 12 like moto.
- **Blocking, second view.** Back on the tracker screen, `$V wait "text~:in Verify Probe app"` (`activity_message`), tap it: `AppTPCompanyTrackersActivity`, `app_name` "Verify Probe", `app_tracked_ago` "Just Now", one `tracking_company_name` per company, matching the `trackerCompanyDisplayName` in the block decisions ("Adjust", "Branch Metrics"). The header there has the same per-app protection switch as My Apps.
- **Inside NetP.** Needs the app signed in to a subscription with the VPN (Settings then has a `VPN` row under "DuckDuckGo Subscription"); only then can `proofs/netp_app_tracking_protection.py` run. It reads NetP's state from the `label` under Settings' `VPN` row, and when it reads "Off", turns NetP on from Settings > VPN (`NetworkProtectionManagementActivity`; dismiss the "Automatically exclude incompatible apps" sheet with `id:autoExcludePromptCancelAction`, then `trailingSwitch`). While the VPN is on, that screen's globe animation keeps `android layout` empty, and right after a reinstall its switch briefly reads off while the VPN is on (tapping it then asks "Is the VPN causing problems with an app?"), so the proof doesn't read it. It turns AppTP on, protects the probe, probes the trackers in `single` and waits 10 s for the kernel's retransmissions. Decisions are logged under `GoBackend`, not `NgVpnNetworkStack`: one `shouldAllowDomain for <host> (<probe uid>) = AppTracker` per connection, then `<host> (<addresses>) already blocked on this connection` for each hello the kernel retransmits on it, before or after the probe closed its socket. An `Owner of <host> (...) is gone (uid 0), using uid <probe uid>` line is a retransmission judged again, and a `shouldAllowDomain for <host> (0) = null` line is a hello that reached the tracker. Debug builds also log NetP's WireGuard configuration, private key included, so the proof saves only the decision lines (`netp-apptp-decisions.txt`). Without the owner fix, one retransmitted hello per tracker was allowed with uid 0 (2026-11-17). With it but before the connection's verdict was remembered, every retransmission was judged again, and so counted on the tracker screen: 11 block decisions per connection (FAIL 9/11); remembering it leaves 1, with 10 retransmissions dropped (PASS 11/11), on 2026-11-18 on netguard's signed-in API 34 emulator. The blocked connection keeps retransmitting with backoff for the 10 s wait.
- **Dev settings.** Settings, then `$V scroll up` one swipe at a time until `text:AppTP dev settings` is visible (it sits among the Internal Features, a few swipes down). Tap it: `VpnInternalSettingsActivity`, "AppTp Dev Settings", with `exceptionRules`, `delete_tracking_history`, `force_update_blocklist`, `protectAllApps`, `unprotectAllApps`, `restoreDefaultAppProtections`, "VPN Debug Logging" (`trailingSwitch`) and `apptp_bugreport`.

## Gotchas

- Submit on the report form sends `m_atp_breakage_report` with the app's package, the category and the user's text to DuckDuckGo (`ReportBreakageCategorySingleChoiceActivity.kt:170`, `DeviceShieldPixels.kt:654`), even from a debug build. Drive the form up to Submit and leave with back.
- Leaving the report form with back still leaves the app's protection off. The "Report an issue?" dialog comes after the switch has already changed.
- Turning an app's protection on or off restarts the VPN (`m_vpn_ev_start_attempt_c` again), but only when the user leaves My Apps, and only if the protections differ from when it opened (`ManageAppsProtectionViewModel.onPause`). Another app coming to the front counts as leaving: a probe started over My Apps right after a toggle has its connection cut (`ERROR:SocketException`). Leave My Apps and wait for `m_vpn_ev_start_attempt_success_c` before probing.
- `dumpsys vpn_management` keeps naming the app as the "Active package" after AppTP is off, so it is no on/off signal. Use `dumpsys connectivity` (`ni{VPN` with the app's `OwnerUid`) or `ip -o link` (`tun<N>`).
- VPN consent survives clearing the app's data. Without the `appops ... ACTIVATE_VPN default` reset, the "Connection request" dialog shows only the first time the app ever asks.
- "Disable and Delete Data" doesn't reset onboarding. It marks the feature removed and deletes tracker history (`VpnFeatureRemover.manuallyRemoveFeature`); only the scheduled removal resets onboarding. The proof cancels it.
- My Apps lists the `android` CLI's own on-device server ("Android CLI Instrumentation Server") as a protected app. The proof skips it when picking an app to toggle. The app it toggles is the user's, so its name goes only into a note: check names, details and errors reach `pr-summary`, which goes into a public PR.
- Verbose AppTP logging: the VPN process registers a receiver while AppTP is on, so `adb shell am broadcast -a logging --es turn on` (or `off`) switches it (`vpn-internal/.../logs/DebugLoggingReceiver.kt`). The dev settings switch does the same. It read on after a data clear on 2026-11-17.
- The "Report Issues" link inside the disabled info panel is a text span. Tapping `infoPanelText`'s centre is not guaranteed to hit it; it was not driven.
- Right after launch, the New Tab Page can show "Let DuckDuckGo protect more of what you do online" (set as default browser). `android layout` lists only its `touch_outside`, so `$V key back` dismisses it. `steps.menu` copes with it by retrying.
- No 16 KB page-size compatibility dialog showed on this 4 KB image. Another image may show one after launch.
- The tracker hosts are live, because AppTP routes only public addresses through its tunnel; a fixture on the host (`10.0.2.2`) or the LAN would bypass it. The blocklist is downloaded at runtime: if a tracker check reads "block decision missing" while logcat has `shouldAllowDomain for <host> (...) = null`, check the domain is still on the list before suspecting the build.
- DNS vs TLS: a tracker the probe sees as `NO_ADDRESS` with the block decision logged was blocked at the DNS lookup, so the TLS server-name blocking under test never ran; the proof fails it as "blocked by DNS, TLS blocking not reached". AppTP routes DNS through its tunnel only when a build sets tunnel DNS (as the removed moto g workaround did).
- An app with protection off in My Apps is routed around the tunnel, so netguard logs nothing for it and its trackers go through (detail "no tunnel session"). The blocking proof turns Verify Probe's protection back on at the start. Without verbose AppTP logging the same detail shows; debug builds log netguard and `shouldAllowDomain` already (the dev settings switch is disabled there), otherwise `adb shell am broadcast -a logging --es turn on`.
- The moto bug: netguard before 1.10.3 scaled the receive window in its SYN-ACK by the app's window scale. Linux picks that scale from the larger of `/proc/sys/net/core/rmem_max` and the last value of `/proc/sys/net/ipv4/tcp_rmem`. The moto g(60) has `rmem_max` 128 MB, so apps advertise scale 12, netguard's 524288-byte window became 128 bytes, the hello went out in 128-byte segments that cut the SNI, and trackers got through. Stock emulators have 256 KB (scale 6), where both versions block, which is why `window` also runs at the moto value when adbd runs as root. The proof never runs `adb root`: it restarts adbd, dropping adb and the recording mid-run, and the device stays rooted until it reboots or `adb unroot`. To verify like-moto, run `adb -s <serial> root` before the proof, on an emulator you may root (google_apis images allow it; Play Store images and production phones don't). Without root, `window like moto` is listed as not verified. A run killed between setting and restoring `rmem_max` leaves 128 MB; a reboot resets it.
- `adb root` and `adb unroot` restart adbd, which drops the port forward to the `android` CLI's on-device server while the server keeps running: every `android layout` then fails with "Error communicating with instrumentation server" (and `$V` with "android layout returned nothing"). `adb shell am force-stop com.android.cli.interact.instrumentation` makes the CLI start a fresh one.
- FAIL before: `netguard-android` 1.10.2 in `versions.properties` on a throwaway local ref (commit it on a scratch branch), then `$V build --base <ref>` and `$P compare .agents/skills/verify-android/proofs/app_tracking_protection_blocking.py`. Expected: `window like moto` trackers allowed before, blocked after; the other modes pass on both.
- The probe is signed with `~/.android/debug.keystore`. One signed on another machine can't be updated, so `$V probe` uninstalls and reinstalls it; the probe is this skill's own app. The proof leaves it installed, so later runs skip the install and My Apps keeps its protection setting.
- Driven with the android CLI on 2026-11-18 (emulator API 34 google_apis, `$V build --base` of this branch, APK `1d8f6b320971`, the app code of `1a85357936`; netguard 1.10.3): `apptp-settings-entry`, `apptp-vpn-consent`, `apptp-enabled`, `apptp-reenable`, `apptp-ntp-card` (its text in both states), `apptp-app-list`, `apptp-report` (both routes, up to Submit), `apptp-having-issues`, `apptp-remove` (Cancel), `apptp-disable` and `apptp-dev-settings` (opened) passed in `proofs/app_tracking_protection.py` (34 checks, via the switch). `apptp-onboarding` and `apptp-empty-feed` passed in it on 2026-11-17 on a fresh app (35 checks via onboarding); this app had onboarded and has history. `apptp-blocking`, `apptp-blocking-late-sni`, `apptp-blocking-like-moto` (adbd already root) and `apptp-blocking-feed` passed in `proofs/app_tracking_protection_blocking.py` (22 checks). With adbd not root it passed 18 checks and listed `window like moto` as not verified; that run also found Verify Probe unprotected and AppTP on, and turned the probe's protection back on and AppTP off first. With `protect_probe` left out and Verify Probe's protection off in My Apps, it was FAIL, 13 of 22 (trackers allowed in every blocking mode, no companies blocked for the probe). Driven by hand only: `apptp-notification`, tapping the New Tab Page card, and "Not Now" on the "Report an issue?" dialog. Not driven: "Disable for One App", "Restore Default Protected Apps", VPN consent Cancel, the FAQ links, the "Report Issues" link, "Disable" in Disable and Delete Data, the dev settings' actions, and the all-protected tracker screen (no app with protection disabled; its text is from `TrackerFeedAdapter`). The FAIL-before route with netguard 1.10.2 was not run (a second full app build).
